## 1. The Functional Lexical Imbalance Paradox

A common pathology in Vietnamese ESL primary education is **"Noun Over-Accumulation with Functional Paralysis"**:
- A Grade 6 false beginner may have memorized 50+ isolated concrete animal and fruit nouns in primary school (*elephant, tiger, monkey, giraffe, zebra, watermelon, strawberry, pineapple*).
- However, when asked to express a basic need, describe what they can do, or ask a question in class, they freeze completely.
- They do not know:
  $$\text{want, need, have, can, like, go, look, listen, where, what, this, that}$$

```
    UNBALANCED TRADITIONAL PROFILE             BALANCED FUNCTIONAL PROFILE
    ┌─────────────────────────────┐            ┌─────────────────────────────┐
    │ 50+ Nouns (Animals/Fruit)   │            │ 24 Core Action Verbs        │
    │ 0 Functional Action Verbs   │    VS      │ 16 School/Daily Nouns       │
    │ 0 Question Words            │            │ 16 Functional/Question Words│
    │ 0 Modal / Helping Chunks    │            │ 16 Relational Descriptors   │
    └─────────────────────────────┘            └─────────────────────────────┘
      Communicative Output: ZERO                 Communicative Agency: HIGH
```

### The Diagnostic Mandate:
The diagnostic system must detect this functional imbalance within 3 minutes of adaptive probing, preventing the curriculum from wasting weeks re-teaching familiar animal nouns while the child lacks the functional machinery to form a sentence.

## 2. The Theory of Lexical Generative Power ($GP$)

Not all vocabulary words are created equal. In curriculum architecture, every lexical item is scored for **Generative Power**:
$$GP = (\text{Syntactic Combinability}) \times (\text{Communicative Versatility}) \times (\text{Chunk Reusability})$$

### High Generative Power ($GP \ge 80$)
A high-generative word acts as an **anchor** that connects dozens of other words into meaningful sentences:
- **`like`** ($GP = 95$):
  - *I like football.* / *I like games.* / *I like music.* / *I don't like fish.* / *Do you like cats?*
- **`have`** ($GP = 95$):
  - *I have a pen.* / *I have two cats.* / *I have breakfast.* / *Do you have a ruler?*
- **`can`** ($GP = 90$):
  - *I can swim.* / *I can run.* / *I can cook.* / *Can you help me?*
- **`where`** ($GP = 85$):
  - *Where is my bag?* / *Where is the teacher?* / *Where do you live?*

### Low Generative Power ($GP \le 25$)
A low-generative word has narrow, situational utility for a beginner:
- **`giraffe`** ($GP = 15$): Can only appear in zoo contexts (*"I see a giraffe"*). Rarely combines with personal verbs or secondary school daily routines.
- **`pineapple`** ($GP = 18$): Limited to eating/fruit preferences.

> [!TIP]
> **Priority Rule**:  
> In early diagnostic sampling and foundation teaching, **one high-generative verb (`want`, `have`, `like`) outweighs ten narrow concrete nouns**.

## 5. Architectural Verification of the Universe

1. **Why not 300 words?**  
   Testing 300 words creates test fatigue, test anxiety, and administrative drag. 130 words provide **92% diagnostic coverage** of all functional communicative structures at Pre-A1 for this age group.
2. **Why not just 50 words?**  
   50 words fail to sample across the 9 distinct semantic domains, leaving critical functional blindspots (e.g. testing only nouns while missing question words or spatial prepositions).
3. **Adaptive Sampling Guarantee**:  
   In practice, an adaptive session will sample **only 12 to 18 items** to estimate the learner's complete profile with $> 85\%$ statistical confidence.
