# Lesson 001 After-Lesson Decision Framework (No Pass/Fail)

> **Purpose**: Guide the teacher/tutor to choose an instructional next step based on the observable pattern of evidence from Lesson 1.  
> **Axiom**: We do NOT assign a numerical percentage or "Pass/Fail" label to an anxious 12-year-old. We select one of four adaptive instructional pathways.

---

## 1. The 4 Adaptive Decision Pathways

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                       EVIDENCE PATTERN TO ACTION                            │
│                                                                             │
│   [ ADVANCE ]             ──► Ready for Lesson 002 directly                 │
│   [ ADVANCE WITH REVIEW ] ──► Proceed to L2 with a 5-min Lesson 1 warm-up   │
│   [ REPEAT PART ]         ──► Re-run only the confused task (ball game)     │
│   [ SIMPLIFY ]            ──► Reduce load to 2 core words; provide choices  │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

---

## 2. Decision Matrix & Concrete Evidence Profiles

### Pathway 1: ADVANCE
- **Evidence Profile**:
  - Task 1 (`My name is...`) is **Independent** or **Modelled** with ease.
  - Task 2 (`I'm 12`) is produced accurately.
  - Task 3 (`What's your name?`) is attempted in the dialogue.
  - Student self-report is **🙂 Dễ & Vui** or **😐 Hơi khó chút**.
- **Teacher Action**:
  - Proceed directly to **Lesson 002 (Things I Like)** at the next session.
- **Concrete Example**:
  > *Minh hesitated initially during the avatar video, but once the word cards were on the table, he placed his name card and said "My name is Minh" with a smile. In the ball game, he asked "What's your name?" with teacher nodding encouragement. He circled 🙂.* $\rightarrow$ **ADVANCE**.

---

### Pathway 2: ADVANCE WITH REVIEW
- **Evidence Profile**:
  - Target chunks are understood and produced with **Prompted** support (e.g. teacher points to card or gives first word *"My..."*).
  - Student dropped one chunk (e.g. forgot age or hesitated on question), but successfully produced their name.
  - Student self-report is **😐 Hơi khó chút** (not distressed).
- **Teacher Action**:
  - Proceed to **Lesson 002**, but dedicate the first 5 minutes of Lesson 2 to a playful name-toss ball game to reinforce Lesson 1 chunks before introducing preferences.
- **Concrete Example**:
  > *Lan needed the teacher to say "My..." before completing "name is Lan". She answered "12" instead of "I'm 12". However, she was cheerful and enjoyed throwing the soft ball. Self-report was 😐.* $\rightarrow$ **ADVANCE WITH REVIEW**.

---

### Pathway 3: REPEAT PART
- **Evidence Profile**:
  - Student learned to say their name, but experienced heavy confusion or hesitation specifically on the interactive question (`What's your name?`).
  - Student self-report is **🙁 Khó quá** or expressed feeling mixed up.
- **Teacher Action**:
  - Do NOT advance to Lesson 2 yet. Schedule a short 15-minute micro-session or spend the first half of the next meeting replaying ONLY the ball-toss game with cards. Do NOT introduce new words.
- **Concrete Example**:
  > *Duc kept mixing up "My name is" and "What's your name?", saying "My name is Duc? What's your name Alex?". He looked frustrated. Self-report was 🙁.* $\rightarrow$ **REPEAT PART** (Replay the role-play card game with puppets to separate statement from question).

---

### Pathway 4: SIMPLIFY
- **Evidence Profile**:
  - Student was unable to produce any chunk independently; remained silent across multiple tasks; required full repetition for every word.
  - Student circled **🙁 Khó quá** or **🛑 Con muốn nghỉ**, showing elevated anxiety.
- **Teacher Action**:
  - Step back immediately. In the next session, eliminate the question chunk completely. Focus exclusively on 2 elements: waving *"Hello"* and saying *"Minh"* while pointing to a name badge. Restore confidence through 100% guaranteed micro-wins before attempting full sentences.
- **Concrete Example**:
  > *Anxious student became completely silent when asked to combine 3 cards. Whispered inaudibly. Looked down and circled 🛑.* $\rightarrow$ **SIMPLIFY** (Do not push full sentences; celebrate single-word badge pointing in Vietnamese first).
