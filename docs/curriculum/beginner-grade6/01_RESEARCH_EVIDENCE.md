# 01. Research Evidence Matrix & Pedagogical Foundations

> **Curriculum Project**: English From Zero — Foundation Curriculum (Grade 6 Vietnam)  
> **Authors**: Curriculum Architect (Role A), Young Learner ESL Specialist (Role B), Vietnamese Learner Specialist (Role C), Learning Science Specialist (Role D), Child Anxiety Specialist (Role E), Synthesis Lead (Role L)  
> **Target Audience**: 11–12-year-old Vietnamese 6th graders at Pre-A1 / Below A1 with high foreign language learning anxiety.

---

## 1. Executive Research Summary

Designing a foundational language curriculum for an 11–12-year-old who has fallen behind requires synthesizing six distinct scientific domains:
1. **International Language Standards**: CEFR Companion Volume (2020) Pre-A1 descriptors for Young Learners (11–15) and Cambridge Young Learners English (Pre-A1 Starters / A1 Movers).
2. **National Curriculum Alignment**: Vietnam MOET Chương trình GDPT 2018 Tiếng Anh Lớp 6 (Global Success / Friends Plus / i-Learn Smart World).
3. **Cognitive Science & Working Memory**: Sweller's Cognitive Load Theory (CLT), element interactivity reduction, and Cowan's working memory capacity ($4 \pm 1$ chunks).
4. **Second Language Acquisition (SLA)**: Formulaic sequences (Wray, 2002), the Lexical Approach (Lewis, 1993), Nation's vocabulary utility frameworks (2001, 2013), and Krashen's Low Affective Filter Hypothesis (1982).
5. **Memory & Retention**: Spaced retrieval practice (Roediger & Karpicke, 2006) and adaptive spaced repetition (FSRS v5).
6. **Contrastive Linguistics**: Vietnamese-English phonological and syntactic transfer interference (syllable-timed vs. stress-timed rhythm, unreleased final stops, zero-coda fricatives, non-inflectional morphology).

## 3. Epistemic Classification: Fact vs. Principle vs. Decision vs. Hypothesis vs. Unknown

In accordance with scientific rigor, all curriculum specifications are classified into five explicit epistemic tiers (detailed in `19_EPISTEMIC_AUDIT.md`):

### Category A: VERIFIED SOURCE FACTS (Ground Truth)
- **VF-01**: Vietnam's Grade 6 Global Success textbook begins Unit 1 with Present Simple, Adverbs of Frequency, and school vocabulary assuming students already know personal pronouns, basic verbs, and phonics.
- **VF-02**: Vietnamese phonotactics does not permit consonant clusters in onset or coda, and final stops (/p, t, k/) are unreleased.
- **VF-03**: Vietnamese grammar has zero morphological agreement (no verb conjugation for person/number, no plural suffixes).
- **VF-04**: LingoPro's existing codebase contains operational FSRS v5, SafeHarbor speech scoring, and S-V-O Sentence Builder primitives.

### Category B: RESEARCH-SUPPORTED PRINCIPLES
- **RP-01**: Spaced retrieval practice improves long-term retention compared to passive massed restudying.
- **RP-02**: Active working memory is constrained ($4 \pm 1$ elements); multi-faceted simultaneous instructions cause element interactivity overload.
- **RP-03**: High classroom evaluation anxiety induces performance avoidance, hesitation, and verbal inhibition in young language learners.
- **RP-04**: Early exposure to invariant formulaic sequences promotes communicative self-efficacy and low-anxiety participation.

### Category C: DESIGN DECISIONS (Initial Pilot Parameters & Heuristics)
- **DD-01**: Replacing explicit grammar formulas (`S + V(s/es)`) with invariant lexical chunks (`I like [X]`, `I play [Y]`) in Lessons 1–6.
- **DD-02**: Setting initial pilot lexical intake to 4–6 productive target items and 1–2 reusable chunks per lesson, with adaptive pacing.
- **DD-03**: Introducing final consonant release (/s, z, t, d, k/) as physical mouth cues ("clean throat tap", "snake whisper") rather than theoretical IPA symbols.
- **DD-04**: SafeHarbor 2.0 re-design: treating ASR transcripts as supportive practice tools and decoupling automated scores from lesson passing gates.

### Category D: PILOT HYPOTHESES (To Test in Pilot)
- **PH-01**: A Grade 6 learner experiencing structured micro-wins and zero cold-calling across Lessons 1–3 will demonstrate observable reductions in avoidance behavior and self-report lower fear of making mistakes.
- **PH-02**: Teaching final /s/ via physical articulatory cues ("snake whisper") before introducing plural -s rules results in observable physical attempts to release coda /s/ on target nouns.
- **PH-03**: Practicing through tactile S-V-O Sentence Builder chips significantly reduces word-order errors (e.g. *I games like*) during scaffolded sentence construction tasks.

### Category E: UNKNOWNS
- **UN-01**: The exact degree of ongoing classroom homework stress and teacher pressure the pilot student experiences in their concurrent formal schooling.
- **UN-02**: Variation in regional Vietnamese dialects (Northern vs. Central vs. Southern) impacting specific vowel perception and coda merger patterns.

## 4. Academic Conflict Resolutions

During the research phase, our specialist agents addressed the following pedagogical debates:

### Conflict 1: When should grammatical rules be introduced?
- **Option A (Traditional Grammar Specialist)**: Teach rules in Lesson 1 (`to be`, personal pronouns table, singular vs plural) to establish correct structural foundations.
- **Option B (SLA & Young Learner Specialists)**: Defer explicit rule explanation until Stage 3; use purely functional chunks in Stages 0–2.
- **Evidence**: Ellis (2005) demonstrates that explicit grammar instruction before learners have built an intuitive receptive repertoire increases cognitive load and error rate without improving spontaneous oral production.
- **Final Decision**: **Option B with Guided Noticing**. Students use phrases communicatively first (`I have a cat`, `He has a dog`), notice patterns inductively in Stage 2, and receive micro-explanations only after achieving productive comfort.

### Conflict 2: Should Vietnamese translation be permitted in the app?
- **Option A (Direct Method / Immersion)**: 100% English immersion, no Vietnamese, rely entirely on pictures.
- **Option B (Bilingual Scaffolding)**: Clear, empathetic Vietnamese translation available on demand.
- **Evidence**: MacIntyre (1999) proves that total immersion for anxious false beginners significantly increases panic and task abandonment. Butman & Krashen (1988) note that comprehensible input is facilitated by rapid L1 glossing.
- **Final Decision**: **Option B with Progressive Fading**. Vietnamese is used for emotional reassurance, contextual framing, and instant glossing in Stages 0–2, then progressively reduced to $\le 20\%$ by Stage 5.
