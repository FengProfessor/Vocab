# 08. Grammar Progression & Inductive Syntax Architecture

> **Curriculum Project**: English From Zero — Foundation Curriculum (Grade 6 Vietnam)  
> **Authors**: Grammar & Usage Specialist (Role G), Vietnamese Learner Specialist (Role C), Synthesis Lead (Role L)  
> **Guiding Philosophy**: **GRAMMAR IS THE SUPPORTING SCAFFOLD, NEVER THE MONUMENT. A child must experience the communicative necessity of a pattern before encountering its formal description.**

---

## 1. Contrastive Grammar Analysis: Why Vietnamese Learners Struggle

Vietnamese is an **isolating, non-inflected, tonal language** with radically different structural properties from English:

| English Grammatical Property | Vietnamese Equivalence | Consequence for Vietnamese 6th Grader |
|---|---|---|
| **Morphological Inflection** (e.g. *play $\rightarrow$ plays / played*) | **Zero Inflection**: Tenses and agreement are indicated by standalone auxiliary words (*đã, đang, sẽ*), never by word mutation. | Learner sees no natural necessity to add *-s* or *-ed* to a verb; drops them entirely unless mechanically forced. |
| **Obligatory Copula Verb `to be`** (*She is happy*) | **Zero Copula with Adjectives**: Adjectives function as stative predicates (*Cô ấy vui*, not *Cô ấy là vui*). | Learner says *"She happy"* or *"I very tired"*, omitting `is/am` because Vietnamese has no linking verb with adjectives. |
| **Dummy Auxiliary `do/does` in Negation & Questions** | **Simple Negation Particle**: Insert *không* directly before the verb (*Tôi không thích*). | Learner produces *"I no like"* or *"I not like"* instead of *"I don't like"*; asks *"You like pizza?"* instead of *"Do you like pizza?"*. |
| **Plural Suffixes `-s/-es`** | **Classifiers & Number Markers**: Add *những, các* or numbers before the noun (*hai con mèo*). | Learner says *"two cat"*; drops plural suffixes because the number *two* already conveys plurality in their native logic. |
| **Strict S-V-O Word Order vs. Topic-Prominent Flexibility** | **Flexible Topic-Comment**: *"Cái này tôi thích"* (Topic first). | Learner translates literally: *"This one I like"* or creates disjointed syntax fragments. |

## 2. The 12-Step Pedagogical Grammar Progression

The sequence below is rigorously ordered to prioritize **immediate communicative utility, low cognitive friction, and gradual morphological exposure**:

```
[1. Lexicalized Identity (I'm...)] 
       │ (Zero-conjugation chunk)
       ▼
[2. Invariant Verb Frames (I like / I don't like)] 
       │ (S-V-O anchor; unanalyzed "don't")
       ▼
[3. Modal Ability (I can / I can't)] 
       │ (Zero inflection; universal across all subjects)
       ▼
[4. Demonstratives & Deixis (This is / That's)] 
       │ (Pointing to immediate physical reality)
       ▼
[5. Possessive Determiners (my / your / his / her)] 
       │ (Marking ownership and relationships)
       ▼
[6. Plural Noun Phonics (-s/-es tied to /s/, /z/, /ɪz/)] 
       │ (Acoustic grounding before spelling rules)
       ▼
[7. Existential Constructions (There is / There are)] 
       │ (Spatial inventory of surroundings)
       ▼
[8. Spatial Prepositions (on, in, under, next to)] 
       │ (Concrete 3D spatial mapping)
       ▼
[9. Present Continuous for Visible Action (be + V-ing)] 
       │ (High visual salience; "right now")
       ▼
[10. Adverbs of Frequency (always, usually, sometimes, never)]
       │ (Habitual frequency anchors)
       ▼
[11. Interrogative Architecture (Wh- & Do you...?)] 
       │ (Social agency & proactive inquiry)
       ▼
[12. 3rd-Person Singular Agreement (He plays / She likes)] 
         (Introduced LATE, only after 1st-person confidence is unshakeable)
```

## 3. Deep Architectural Specification per Grammar Item

### Item G-01: Invariant Identity Frame (`I'm...`)
- **Prerequisite**: None. Taught in Lesson 001.
- **Communicative Purpose**: Self-introduction (name, age, status) without terrifying the learner with `am/is/are` conjugation tables.
- **Example Chunks**: `I'm Minh.`, `I'm 12 years old.`, `I'm a student.`
- **Pedagogical Treatment**: Treat `I'm` as a single indivisible lexical block /aɪm/, not as pronoun + verb.
- **Typical Vietnamese Error**: Saying *"I Minh"* or *"My name Nam"*.
- **Empathetic Correction**: *"Tuyệt vời! Con nhớ thêm từ đệm 'I'm' vào nhé: 'I'm Minh' nghe sẽ rất tây và tự nhiên!"*

### Item G-02: S-V-O Verb Frames & Negation (`I like / I don't like`)
- **Prerequisite**: G-01. Taught in Lesson 002.
- **Communicative Purpose**: Expressing immediate desires and emotional stance.
- **Example Chunks**: `I like football.`, `I don't like fish.`, `I play games.`
- **Pedagogical Treatment**: Use the S-V-O Sentence Builder. Color-code components: Blue (I), Orange (like/don't like), Green (music/games).
- **Typical Vietnamese Error**: *"I no like fish"* or *"I not like"*.
- **Empathetic Correction**: Show two puzzle pieces: `[I]` + `[don't like]` + `[fish]`. Point out: *"Trong tiếng Anh, 'không thích' là nguyên một khối: 'don't like'!"*

### Item G-03: Invariant Modal Ability (`can / can't`)
- **Prerequisite**: G-02. Taught in Lesson 003.
- **Communicative Purpose**: Expressing personal capabilities and limits safely.
- **Example Chunks**: `I can swim.`, `I can't dance.`, `Can you run fast?`
- **Pedagogical Treatment**: High-utility modal with **zero conjugation** across any person (`I can, you can, he can, they can`). Builds instant grammatical confidence.
- **Typical Vietnamese Error**: Saying *"I can to swim"* or *"I am can swim"*.
- **Empathetic Correction**: *"Từ 'can' đã có sức mạnh siêu nhân rồi, không cần thêm 'to' hay 'am' gì nữa đâu con nhé: 'I can swim' là xong!"*

### Item G-04: Spatial Deixis (`This is / That's`)
- **Prerequisite**: G-01, G-02. Taught in Lesson 005 & 012.
- **Communicative Purpose**: Sharing physical attention and pointing out objects near and far.
- **Example Chunks**: `This is my bag.`, `That's my school.`, `What's this?`
- **Typical Vietnamese Error**: Omitting `is` $\rightarrow$ *"This my bag"*; confusing distance.
- **Empathetic Correction**: Physical gestures. Touch the table: *"This is my pen."* Point to the door: *"That is the door."*

### Item G-05: Possessive Determiners (`my / your / his / her`)
- **Prerequisite**: G-04. Taught in Lesson 005, 009, 013.
- **Communicative Purpose**: Expressing ownership and relationships.
- **Example Chunks**: `This is my friend.`, `His name is Nam.`, `Her bag is blue.`
- **Typical Vietnamese Error**: Using subject pronouns as possessives $\rightarrow$ *"He bag is blue"* or *"I bag"*.
- **Empathetic Correction**: Two-column color match: `He $\rightarrow$ His`, `She $\rightarrow$ Her`.

### Item G-06: Plural Suffixes (`-s / -es`)
- **Prerequisite**: G-02, G-05. Taught in Lesson 006 & 010.
- **Communicative Purpose**: Distinguishing single items from multiple items.
- **Example Chunks**: `one book $\rightarrow$ two books`, `three pens`, `five boxes`.
- **Pedagogical Treatment**: **Phonetics First, Spelling Second**. Connect plural `-s` directly to the "snake sound" /s/ or /z/.
- **Typical Vietnamese Error**: Dropping the final sound $\rightarrow$ pronouncing *"two book"*.
- **Empathetic Correction**: *"Bàn tay có 2 quyển sách, cái đuôi của từ cũng phải có tiếng xì hơi: 'two books'!"*

### Item G-07: Existential Sentences (`There is / There are`)
- **Prerequisite**: G-05, G-06. Taught in Lesson 010.
- **Communicative Purpose**: Describing rooms, inventories, and scene compositions.
- **Example Chunks**: `There is a bed in my room.`, `There are two chairs.`
- **Typical Vietnamese Error**: Literal translation from Vietnamese *Có một cái bàn* $\rightarrow$ saying *"Have a table"*.
- **Empathetic Correction**: Clarify: *"Từ 'Have' chỉ dùng khi con hoặc ai đó sở hữu cái gì đó ('I have a pen'). Khi nói 'ở đó có...', tiếng Anh dùng cặp bài trùng: 'There is' (cho 1) và 'There are' (cho nhiều)!"*

### Item G-08: Spatial Prepositions (`on, in, under, next to`)
- **Prerequisite**: G-04, G-07. Taught in Lesson 016.
- **Communicative Purpose**: Locating lost belongings and giving positions.
- **Example Chunks**: `The cat is under the table.`, `My pen is in my bag.`
- **Pedagogical Treatment**: Tactile screen drag-and-drop. Student drags a cat into a box, under a table, or on a bed.

### Item G-09: Present Continuous for Visible Action (`be + V-ing`)
- **Prerequisite**: G-01, G-07. Taught in Lesson 022.
- **Communicative Purpose**: Describing what is happening right now in images or live reality.
- **Example Chunks**: `I am playing games.`, `She is reading a book.`
- **Typical Vietnamese Error**: Dropping `be` $\rightarrow$ *"She reading"* or dropping `-ing` $\rightarrow$ *"She is read"*.
- **Empathetic Correction**: The "Two-Hand Rule": Left hand is `is/am/are`, right hand is `-ing`. They must clap together: `is` + `reading`!

### Item G-10: Adverbs of Frequency (`always, usually, sometimes, never`)
- **Prerequisite**: G-02, G-09. Taught in Lesson 023.
- **Communicative Purpose**: Expressing regularity of habits and routines.
- **Example Chunks**: `I always do my homework.`, `I never drink coffee.`
- **Pedagogical Treatment**: Visual frequency bar from $100\%$ (`always`) to $0\%$ (`never`). Always placed **between** Subject and Verb.

### Item G-11: Interrogative Architecture (`Wh-` and `Do you...?`)
- **Prerequisite**: G-02, G-03, G-08. Taught in Lessons 025 to 030.
- **Communicative Purpose**: Proactive conversational questioning and conducting surveys.
- **Example Chunks**: `Where is my bag?`, `Who is that?`, `Do you like football? - Yes, I do.`
- **Pedagogical Treatment**: Intonation choreography. Wh-questions drop at the end ($\searrow$); Yes/No questions rise at the end ($\nearrow$).

### Item G-12: 3rd-Person Singular Agreement (`He plays / She likes`)
- **Prerequisite**: All prior items G-01 through G-11. Taught in Lesson 040 (School Bridge Stage).
- **Communicative Purpose**: Mastering school test grammar and speaking accurately about others.
- **Example Chunks**: `Nam likes music.`, `My brother plays badminton.`
- **Pedagogical Treatment**: Contrastive spotlight: `I like` (no sound) vs `Nam likes` (add snake sound /s/).
- **Typical Vietnamese Error**: Omission of *-s* in writing and speaking.
- **Empathetic Correction**: *"Khi chủ ngữ là 1 người đặc biệt (He, She, hoặc tên 1 người), động từ phải đội thêm chiếc mũ /s/!"*
