# 15. Teacher & Facilitator Implementation Guide

> **Curriculum Project**: English From Zero — Foundation Curriculum (Grade 6 Vietnam)  
> **Authors**: Young Learner ESL Specialist (Role B), Child Anxiety Specialist (Role E), Synthesis Lead (Role L)  
> **Primary Audience**: Secondary School English Teachers, Tutors, and Remedial Center Instructors.

---

## 1. The Teacher's Emotional Stance: Safety Before Syntax

When teaching an 11–12-year-old who has fallen behind in English, your primary role during the first 6 lessons is **not that of a judge or grammar enforcer, but that of an emotional safety anchor**.

### The 3 Core Rules of Facilitation:
1. **Never Cold-Call**: Never point to an anxious student unexpectedly and demand an answer. Always announce the question first, model an answer with a confident peer, give 10 seconds of private processing time, and provide 2 clear options.
2. **Normalize Approximations**: If a student says *"I like football"* without a crisp /k/ or with a slight accent, **celebrate the communication first**: *"Thầy hiểu ngay con thích bóng đá! Rất tuyệt!"* Then, gently invite an echo: *"Con thử nghe thầy bật nhẹ âm /k/ này: like... football. Thử lại cùng thầy nhé!"*
3. **Praise Strategy, Not Inherent Talent**: Avoid saying *"Con thông minh lắm"*. Instead, say: *"Thầy thấy con đã rất cố gắng ghép đúng 3 từ theo thứ tự S-V-O hôm nay!"*

## 2. Managing the 45-Minute Lesson Structure

```
[00:00 - 00:05] Physical Warm-Up: Smile, stretch, breathe. Zero English tests.
[00:05 - 00:10] Retrieval Warm-Up: Reactivate 3 words from last session using flashcard game.
[00:10 - 00:18] Comprehensible Input: Tell a visual micro-story; play slow audio (0.8x).
[00:18 - 00:25] Guided Recognition: Minimal pair ear-training & physical mouth gestures.
[00:25 - 00:33] S-V-O Builder: Hands-on word chip puzzle on board or tablet screen.
[00:33 - 00:40] Communicative Task: Pair survey or roleplay using structured frames.
[00:40 - 00:45] Can-Do Celebration: Every child stands up and claims their Can-Do badge!
```

## 3. Managing Mixed-Ability Classrooms (Fast vs. Slow Learners)

In real Vietnamese classrooms, you will have students like "Minh" (frozen, zero) sitting next to students like "Duc" (reads fast, gaming vocab).

### The Dual-Track Classroom Strategy:
- **For the Anxious/Struggling Learner (Minh)**:
  - Provide pre-printed Word Bank chips on their desk.
  - Allow them to point or arrange cards before speaking.
  - Give them the role of "Sound Inspector" (holding up the snake card when they hear /s/).
- **For the Advanced/Fast Learner (Duc)**:
  - Do NOT give them 30 extra boring grammar exercises.
  - Challenge them to add an adjective slot: *"Instead of 'I like games', make it 'I like exciting games'!"*
  - Appoint them as "Peer Coach" to model dialogues with high-fives.

## 4. What To Do When a Child Freezes

If a student goes silent, blushes, or lowers their head:
1. **De-escalate Immediately**: Lower your physical height to eye level, smile warmly, and speak softly in Vietnamese: *"Không sao cả con ơi, từ này hơi lạ một chút. Thầy con mình cùng làm nhé."*
2. **Offer a Binary Choice**: *"Con nghĩ là 'I play football' hay 'I eat football' nhỉ?"* (Humor instantly breaks tension).
3. **Shared Choral Chanting**: Have the entire room repeat the sentence together so the individual student does not feel spotlighted.
