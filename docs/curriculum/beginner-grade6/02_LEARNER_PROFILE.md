# 02. Learner Profile & Psychological Architecture

> **Curriculum Project**: English From Zero — Foundation Curriculum (Grade 6 Vietnam)  
> **Authors**: Child Motivation / Learning Anxiety Specialist (Role E), Vietnamese Learner Specialist (Role C), Synthesis Lead (Role L)  
> **Core Principle**: **TUỔI ≠ TRÌNH ĐỘ NGÔN NGỮ (Age $\neq$ Linguistic Proficiency).** A 12-year-old at Pre-A1 is not a 6-year-old child and must be treated with dignity, emotional safety, and age-appropriate content.

---

## 1. Primary Learner Persona: The Anxious Grade 6 Beginner

| Attribute | Profile Specification | Pedagogical Implications |
|---|---|---|
| **Chronological Age** | 11–12 years old | Cognitive maturity: operational thinking, self-conscious, sensitive to peer judgment, aware of social status. |
| **School Environment** | Grade 6 (Lớp 6), Secondary School (THCS), Vietnam | Shift from primary school (play-based, low pressure) to secondary school with formal periodic tests, grammar grading, and public board-work. |
| **Linguistic Level** | Pre-A1 / Below A1 ("Mất gốc" / False Beginner) | Cannot construct a simple spontaneous sentence; recognizes a few isolated words; cannot decode basic spoken English. |
| **Affective State** | High anxiety, low self-efficacy, task avoidance | Pauses or hesitates when called upon; anticipates errors; perceives English as an arbitrary, high-pressure subject. |
| **Prior Negative Experiences** | Grammar tests, unexpected public questioning, low test marks | Demonstrates performance anxiety, extended response latency, and avoidance when confronted with English tasks. |

> [!CAUTION]
> **Strict Pedagogical Axiom**:
> Under NO circumstances should an educator or algorithm label this student as *"lười biếng"* (lazy), *"kém thông minh"* (unintelligent), or *"không có khiếu ngoại ngữ"* (lacking language aptitude). 
> Their difficulty is the predictable outcome of **premature cognitive overload, element interactivity explosion, and systemic lack of phonological and formulaic foundations**.

       ▲                                                          │

       ▲                                                              │

## 3. The 3 Detailed Simulation Personas

To validate curriculum effectiveness across real-world variations in Vietnamese classrooms, we establish three benchmark simulation personas:

### Persona A: "Minh" — The Apprehensive Beginner (High Task Anxiety, Limited Prior English Output)
- **Age**: 12 years old (Grade 6).
- **Background**: Studied English in primary school through passive worksheets; rarely spoke aloud; scored 3.5/10 in first Grade 6 mid-term test.
- **Current Behavior**: Demonstrates visible tension when called upon; lowers gaze; hesitates to attempt unprompted English speech; reports: *"Con sợ nói sai, con thấy tiếng Anh khó quá"*.
- **Skill Profile**:
  - Listening: Minimal (recognizes high-frequency greetings like *hello*, *bye*).
  - Speaking: Very limited (avoids unprompted speech; requires explicit binary choices).
  - Reading: Emergent (identifies familiar Latin letters, decodes isolated familiar words).
  - Syntax: Undeveloped (does not spontaneously combine words into complete subject-verb structures).
- **Curriculum Need**: Needs **Stage 0 (English is Safe)**, zero-threat listening tasks, binary choice selections, and structured rehearsal before any oral output.

### Persona B: "Linh" — The Isolated-Noun Speaker (Persistent L1 Word-Order Transfer)
- **Age**: 11.5 years old (Grade 6).
- **Background**: Enthusiastic flashcard learner in primary school; knows 300+ vocabulary words (animals, fruits, colors, stationery); parents bought many vocab apps.
- **Current Behavior**: When asked *"What do you like?"*, she responds with disconnected nouns: *"Pizza... cat... pink"*. When asked to write a sentence, she reflects Vietnamese word order: *"I pizza eat like"* or *"She is friend my"*.
- **Skill Profile**:
  - Vocabulary Recognition: Moderate (identifies isolated nouns with picture support).
  - Vocabulary Recall: Cued recall of concrete objects.
  - Syntax & Grammar: Persistent L1 transfer (translates word-for-word from Vietnamese).
  - Speaking: Utters isolated words without connecting verbs.
- **Curriculum Need**: Needs **Sentence Builder S-V-O**, high-utility verb engines (`want`, `like`, `have`, `play`), and invariant phrase frames to assemble her dormant nouns into functional communication.

### Persona C: "Duc" — The Silent Visual Reader (Auditory Decoding & Coda Omission Pattern)
- **Age**: 12 years old (Grade 6).
- **Background**: Plays mobile games (Roblox, Minecraft); can read simple English text in games; scores 6.0/10 on written grammar quizzes by memorizing formulas.
- **Current Behavior**: Finds spoken English difficult to segment at natural speed; pronounces *like* as /laɪ/, *games* as /geɪm/, *watch* as /wɒ/; has difficulty discriminating vowel length contrasts (e.g. *ship* vs *sheep*).
- **Skill Profile**:
  - Reading Comprehension: Basic written decoding of game-related texts.
  - Grammar Formula Manipulation: Moderate on written fill-in-the-blank tests (`am/is/are`).
  - Listening Comprehension: Low in fast connected speech.
  - Pronunciation & Intelligibility: Regularly omits English final consonants (/s, z, t, d, k/); applies syllable-timed Vietnamese speech rhythm.
- **Curriculum Need**: Needs **Acoustic Ear-Training**, physical mouth awareness for final consonants (/s, z, t, d, k/), and dual-speed audio priming before reading text.

## 4. Emotional Safety Protocol: The 10 Inviolable Rules

Every lesson and digital activity within the curriculum must comply with the following emotional safety standards:

1. **NO COLD CALLING**: The student is never prompted to speak without prior listening rehearsal and guided practice.
2. **RECOGNITION BEFORE RECALL**: Always allow the student to identify/select before forcing them to retrieve from memory.
3. **BINARY & TERNARY CHOICE SCAFFOLDING**: When in doubt, provide 2–3 clear options rather than an empty blank.
4. **PHYSICAL NOT METALINGUISTIC PHONETICS**: Teach sounds through physical sensations ("puff of air", "snake hissing") instead of IPA phonetic terminology.
5. **ZERO PENALTY FOR PHONETIC APPROXIMATION**: In Stage 0–1, intelligibility and communicative intent outrank native perfection.
6. **NO CONCURRENT ERROR FLOODING**: Never correct more than ONE target linguistic feature at a time.
7. **NO PUBLIC RANKING / LEADERBOARDS**: Avoid competitive leaderboards that display low scores publicly; use personal streak and self-referenced mastery growth.
8. **PRIVATE SAFE-HARBOR AUDIO DRILLS**: Speech recording must allow unlimited retries in private before submission.
9. **AUTHENTIC PERSONALIZATION WITH FICTIONAL FREEDOM**: If a personal question is asked (*"What pets do you have?"*), always allow fictional choices (*"I have a tiger!"*) so learners are not constrained by personal reality.
10. **TANGIBLE "CAN-DO" MICRO-CERTIFICATES**: Every lesson concludes with an explicit, visible communicative achievement: *"Hôm nay con đã nói được 3 món ăn con thích bằng tiếng Anh!"*
