# Pedagogical Manifesto: Beginner Learning Rules (LingoPro Source of Truth)

> **Document Class**: Persistent System Policy & Pedagogical Rulebook  
> **Applies to**: All future AI coding agents, curriculum designers, and software engineers working on LingoPro.  
> **Target Learners**: Pre-A1 / A1 / False Beginners, especially Vietnamese Young Learners (Ages 10–14).  
> **Authority**: This document serves as the inviolable pedagogical source of truth. Code implementations, database schemas, and AI content generators MUST comply with these rules.

---

## 1. The Core Law of Young Learner Pedagogy

$$\mathbf{TUỔI \neq TRÌNH\ ĐỘ\ NGÔN\ NGỮ\ (Age \neq Linguistic\ Proficiency)}$$

A 12-year-old at Pre-A1 is an adolescent with operational cognitive abilities, personal dignity, and emerging teenage interests. They are NOT a 6-year-old child and MUST NOT be subjected to preschool babyish materials. Conversely, they are NOT an adult and MUST NOT be subjected to corporate office scenarios or dense abstract grammar treatises.

---

## 2. The NEVER Rules (Strict Violations)

Future agents and engineers must **NEVER**:

1. **NEVER use raw word-count as the primary curriculum metric.**
   - Reject claims like: *"We built a curriculum of 3,000 beginner words."*
   - Memorizing a 3,000-word list produces isolated flashcard recognition, not communicative competence. Progress must always be measured in observable **Can-Do Competencies**.

2. **NEVER dump deductive grammar formulas before communicative exposure.**
   - NEVER open a beginner lesson with: `S + V(s/es) + O`, *"Thì hiện tại đơn dùng để..."*, or abstract tables of `to be`.
   - Grammar must always follow: **Real Example $\rightarrow$ Meaning $\rightarrow$ Pattern Noticing $\rightarrow$ Micro-Explanation $\rightarrow$ Active Use $\rightarrow$ Spaced Retrieval**.

3. **NEVER equate chronological age with language level.**
   - NEVER generate preschool content (*"Cat, dog, duck, ball"* nursery rhymes) for a 6th grader.
   - NEVER generate adult workplace content (business contracts, job interviews, booking hotels) for a 6th grader.

4. **NEVER create large vocabulary intake batches for beginners.**
   - NEVER introduce more than **6–8 target productive vocabulary items** in a single lesson.
   - Cowan's working memory limit ($4 \pm 1$ chunks) must be strictly respected. 10 words used 20 times is infinitely superior to 30 words seen once.

5. **NEVER abandon prior vocabulary after its introductory lesson.**
   - Every lexical item and formulaic chunk must be deliberately recycled in subsequent lessons via the **Macro Review Graph** and LingoPro's native FSRS v5 engine.

6. **NEVER use public shaming, cold-calling, or red failure banners.**
   - Avoid countdown timers that induce panic in beginners.
   - Avoid public leaderboards based purely on test scores.
   - Never display a screen with 5 red crosses and a "0/10 FAILED" notification.

7. **NEVER claim pedagogical validation without empirical evidence.**
   - NEVER certify a curriculum or lesson as "mastered" merely because an LLM generated it or a user tapped a multiple-choice option once.
   - Validation requires multi-modal evidence across listening, S-V-O syntax assembly, and SafeHarbor speech scoring.

## 3. The MUST Rules (Mandatory System Invariants)

Future agents and engineers **MUST**:

1. **MUST define every lesson around an observable Can-Do outcome.**
   - Every lesson must answer: *"What can the child DO in English at the end of this 45-minute session?"* (e.g. *"I can introduce my best friend"*).

2. **MUST anchor progression through invariant formulaic chunks.**
   - Build early fluency on high-utility lexical frames: `I like...`, `I don't like...`, `I can...`, `I have...`, `Can you help me?`.

3. **MUST address Vietnamese learner L1 phonological interference directly.**
   - Systematically teach the audible release of final consonants (/s, z, t, d, k/) and the distinction between short and long vowels (/ɪ/ vs /iː/).
   - Use physical articulatory mnemonics ("Snake sound", "Puff of air") rather than abstract IPA transcription charts.

4. **MUST cure "Syntax Blindness" with tactile S-V-O Sentence Builders.**
   - Provide color-coded syntactic slots (`Subject` = Blue, `Verb` = Orange, `Object` = Green) to eliminate word-by-word Vietnamese literal translation.

5. **MUST enforce the 8-Level Confidence Ladder.**
   - Progress from receptive listening $\rightarrow$ selection $\rightarrow$ guided repetition $\rightarrow$ slot completion $\rightarrow$ communicative transfer.
   - Never demand open-ended oral production without prior auditory priming and guided rehearsal.

6. **MUST provide an explicit bridge to the national school textbook.**
   - Remediate foundational panic while progressively aligning with MOET Grade 6 (Global Success Units 1–3) to ensure real-world school success.

7. **MUST enforce FSRS v5 spaced retrieval with intra-day learning steps.**
   - Configure FSRS parameters: `request_retention = 0.92`, `learning_steps = ['10m', '4h', '1d']`, and `relearning_steps = ['10m', '30m']`.

## 4. The SHOULD Rules (Best Practices)

Future agents and engineers **SHOULD**:

1. **SHOULD allow fictional and humorous answers in personalization.**
   - Praise a student who says *"I have a pet dinosaur"* for correct grammatical structure rather than penalizing them for realism.

2. **SHOULD maintain a clear distinction between Receptive and Productive lexicon.**
   - Allow a receptive pool of 350–400 words in listening/reading while keeping active productive output focused on a razor-sharp core of 180–220 words.

3. **SHOULD provide an on-demand bilingual emergency parachute.**
   - Keep Vietnamese available as a warm, comforting scaffold in Stages 0–2, progressively fading it as English confidence solidifies.

4. **SHOULD pilot every major curriculum change on small human cohorts.**
   - Follow the 3-phase pilot progression: $N=1$ single learner probe $\rightarrow$ $N=10$ micro-cohort $\rightarrow$ $N=50$ classroom pilot.

## 5. P4R-GS Recalibrated Young Learner Rules (Grade 6 Alignment Invariants)

Enacted under Phase P4R-GS to govern all secondary false-beginner curricula:

1. **Rule 1 — Never Confuse Weak English with Weak Cognition**:
   - A 12-year-old false beginner has developing teenage reasoning, social self-awareness, and problem-solving capability. Never equate Pre-A1 English with preschool intelligence.
2. **Rule 2 — Low Floor, High Ceiling (Đáy ngôn ngữ thấp — Trần nhận thức cao)**:
   - Provide minimal linguistic barriers for entry, but immediately provide rich strategic, logical, and creative missions.
3. **Rule 3 — Move Upward When Trivial Content is Mastered (Rapid Upward Adaptation)**:
   - Two consecutive unprompted passes on Level F must immediately bypass remaining trivial nouns and advance to contrastive, syntactic, or stretch challenges. Never trap a learner at the bottom.
4. **Rule 4 — Global Success is the School-Alignment Spine**:
   - Every theme, lexical cluster, and grammatical milestone must map directly to the national MOET textbook (*Tiếng Anh 6 — Global Success*) to ensure immediate survival and confidence in public school.
5. **Rule 5 — LingoPro Repairs Prerequisites Rather Than Replacing the Textbook**:
   - LingoPro is an intelligent diagnostic and prerequisite-repair engine that sits alongside the school textbook; it does not duplicate or replace classroom teaching.
6. **Rule 6 — Emotionally Safe, Epistemically Strict**:
   - Never use red failure banners, cold-calling, or public shaming. However, never award false credit or blur learning gaps. True compassion lies in accurate diagnosis and targeted repair.
7. **Rule 7 — Related Wrong Answers Remain Useful Evidence But Are Not Exact Mastery**:
   - If a learner says *"pencil"* when shown a pen, acknowledge the semantic connection warmly, but log an exact gap (`active_gap = PEN_PENCIL_CONTRAST`) and deploy a contrastive bridge.
8. **Rule 8 — No Preschoolification of Grade-6 Learners**:
   - Reject soft-toy tossing, cartoon baby stickers, nursery rhymes, and mechanical flashcard chanting for 11–13 year olds. Replace them with authentic missions, secret-agent puzzles, and student-created identity artifacts.
